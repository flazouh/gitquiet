A repository's issues, three ways to group them

The page is /flowline-labs/flowline/issues, drawn with that repository's real rows, read from GitHub this morning: 303 issues, 192 of them open, ten to a page over 31 pages, and 15 assigned to you. Every row below is the issue row gitquiet already draws on Home. What changes between the three is only what stands above the rows.

A. One list, newest first

1 request per page

No headings. The rows are in GitHub's own order and the count and the page sit where a repository's pull request list already puts them. Nothing is claimed about who owes what, because a repository's whole issue list is not owed to you and nothing in this read says otherwise.

B. Courts, as on Home

4 requests per page

Your move holds what is assigned to you, Waiting holds what you raised or were named in, Settled holds what is closed. The trouble is the rest: the search that lists a repository's issues does not say why you are involved in any of them, so the three scoped searches below are extra reads, and the 260-odd rows that answer none of them have no Court to sit in. They are drawn under a fourth heading that means nothing more than "the rest".

C. Open, then Closed

1 request per page

Two headings, from the one fact every row already carries. It reads cleanly on a page where both appear, and on most pages it does not: this repository's first four pages are open issues and nothing else, so the Closed heading is usually absent and the Open heading sits over the whole page saying what the tab above it already said.
